# Service level for a grocery wholesaler

The customer is a wholesaler that resupplies regional supermarket chains. Here the customer measures our service level on every purchase order, and short shipments are charged back to us.

## What I do

- **Reconcile the wholesaler's purchase forecasts against the orders that actually arrive,** snapshot by snapshot, so a gap shows up before the purchase order does. The forecast on this account tends to move late and sharply, inside our lead time, which is the usual cause of a short shipment here.
- **Automate forecast updates into the ERP upload format,** so a revised customer forecast reaches supply planning without retyping.
- **Root-cause short shipments and adjust supply** as revised customer forecasts come in, rather than waiting for the next report to show the gap.
- **Flag supply gaps to the customer early.** It is better to agree on a short shipment in advance than to explain one afterwards.

## Result

| Measure | Value | How it was measured |
|---|---|---|
| Fill rate, five quarters | **about 96%** | units shipped ÷ units ordered, by requested delivery month · every quarter I have managed the account, Q3 2025 through August 2026 · our own order records |
| Inbound fill rate, customer's report | **96.5%** | received ÷ order quantity at close · purchase orders closed in Q2 2026 across three distribution centers · the customer's vendor-performance report |
| Performance charge-backs | **0.03%** of order value | same report, same quarter |

- Two independent sources agree: for Q2 2026 our own order data gives 96.6% and the customer's report gives 96.5%.
- This is a level held over time, not a turnaround. Individual months move with the customer's forecast changes; the quarters stay in a narrow band.
