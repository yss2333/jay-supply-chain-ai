# About

I am a demand planner who builds the forecasting systems I plan with.

I have planned demand at Kiss Beauty Group, a CPG beauty manufacturer, since January 2024. I started with six US and UK accounts, and today I cover 20+ retail accounts across the US, Canada and Europe, about 1,400 nail SKUs. I joined as a junior demand planning data analyst and was promoted to demand planning specialist in January 2026. In that time the job changed under me twice: first I automated the reporting around it, then a colleague and I replaced the forecasting engine itself.

## How the work grew

| Year | What I was building | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | A seasonality index in R; a forecast-accuracy dashboard in Power BI | We could finally see how wrong we were, by account and by month |
| 2024–25 | Data pipelines from SAP into Snowflake; order, shipment and POS models | One place to ask a question, instead of four exports |
| 2025 | Forecast automation in Python; freight routing and stock checks as tools | The weekly work stopped being manual |
| 2026 | A multi-agent forecasting system with a rule engine and a validation process | The engine we run on now, and the gate that decides what ships |

The through-line is not the tools. It is that every year the answer to "how do you know that number is right?" got better.

## What I do

**Demand planning.** Weekly forecast review across the US, Canada and Europe as the demand input to S&OP, new product introduction (NPI) forecasting, and allocation when supply is short.

**Systems.** The forecasting engine, the review agents, the excess and aging inventory reporting, freight routing, supplier coverage reports, and the dashboards planners and buyers use.

**Validation.** Unit tests, backtests against a naive baseline, regression and shadow comparisons. It is the part of the work I care most about, because it is the part that decides whether an improvement is real.
