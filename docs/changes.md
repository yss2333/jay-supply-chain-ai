**September 21** — [Freight routing](work/shipping-route-automation.md) was filled out with what the page had been missing. The scope is stated: five plants across the US, Canada and Europe, each carrying its own transit time rather than a shared default, with the UK lane given an extra week for being the longest and least predictable. The scale is stated: sixteen buyers run it two or three times a week, on the order of 130 cycles and 39,000 lines a year, with about 7% coming back for a person to decide — the point being not the 93% but where a buyer's attention goes. And the confirmed-mode figure is read a second way, as a service answer rather than a cost one: 99.4% of the lines sent by a cheaper lane were never rescued by air, so spending less did not create emergencies — with the limit stated, that not being rescued is not the same as arriving before the stockout, which was not measured. A run-time figure went up during the day and came down again: it varies by buyer, so there is no single number to state. No existing number changed; the hours-saved and dollar-saving figures remain unclaimed.
